Technical correctness, truthful claims and artistic quality are separate decisions. A file that decodes correctly can still be poor film design. Agreement between reviewers is not artistic evidence.

## Distinguish layout from finished art

A schematic establishes hierarchy, copy and space. Gray bars, named boxes, generic icons, missing imagery/materials and a label such as “focal action” do not establish finished graphic quality. Keep those artifacts labeled as schematics. Do not promote a readable layout into a finished style-frame gate simply because its text fits.

A finished style frame uses the intended subject, typography, image assets, color field, material/light and compositing treatment at the proposed delivery size. Pure graphics or a type-only composition can be finished art; 3D, photographic imagery, glass, grain, gradients and particles are not compulsory. The issue is whether the actual composition performs the idea and has a distinctive, deliberate visual language.

Before a full cut, show finished visual evidence for the opening, a consequential proof/explanatory moment and an adjacent destination from one world. Include the demanding intermediate state and settled poster where they differ. Then make a real short motion passage using those assets and that finish. A series of stills does not prove camera feel or movement.

## Decide whether the image earns production

The director and critics judge the actual candidate on these questions:

- **Image premise:** what subject or governing graphic gesture is visible before reading the heading? Does it express the intended change, or merely decorate a claim?
- **Authored composition:** do type scale, line breaks, crop, negative space and optical relationships serve this shot? Repeating heading-plus-box page furniture is not by itself an editorial direction.
- **World and finish:** do background, foreground, material/light and texture belong together? Adding complexity or a fashionable material cannot rescue a weak subject.
- **Specificity:** does a team claim show coordinated decisions affecting one film, a camera claim reveal a useful relationship, and a review claim make seeing the whole edit useful? A role inventory or the word “result” is not a performed consequence.
- **Sequence:** do adjacent shots vary viewpoint, scale, color or density for a reason while retaining identity? Does the motif change role or meaning?
- **Small display:** does the essential meaning survive the intended viewing width? Secondary microdetail may be texture; it cannot carry a necessary claim.

Use a stage decision, not an average score: **hold** for an absent core subject, schematics passed off as finished art, a material visual gap from the selected references, or a route unable to realize the promised image; **revise** for a bounded weakness in a convincing direction; **ready for the next named stage** only when the actual evidence supports it. No effect quota, model vote or technical pass cancels a core creative failure. These are production decisions, not new user-permission requirements.

## Motion and cut acceptance

Prove departure, transformation/camera action and readable arrival with the real assets and finish. Inspect original-speed playback plus consecutive contact frames. Movement should express cause or perspective, lead attention and settle on meaning. Test the full middle-state envelope, including overshoot, blur, shadows, open overlays and moving type. A page fade, spinning empty frame or undirected push-in alone does not demonstrate cinematic motion design.

Judge cadence across the actual cut. An offstage entrance with a slow ease-in can create an unintended empty pause; inspect the first visible motion rather than only the arrival. A result needs time to read, but an extended hold may need a further meaningful action or an earlier cut. Changing pose continuously is not a substitute for either choice. A closing convergence can earn a settled read; it need not keep drifting to prove motion.

Check every authored stop-motion pose for contrast and word visibility: a good poster does not establish the intervening variants. Align narration with the visible action or relationship it names, and review that timing again after reordering shots. In an explanatory sequence, preserve the causal progression from problem through mechanism to consequence; showing clarity and then introducing unexplained noise can reverse the intended argument.

For the full cut, ask independent critics to identify the weakest actual shot and whether it lowers the film's visual standard. Supply the brief and reference comparison, not only a list of tiny defects already found by the author. A focused correction review verifies that correction; it does not renew unsupplied full-cut artistic acceptance.

Record the exact candidate/reference files and cells, stage, viewing scale, observed gap, proposed correction, disagreement and director decision. Keep clipping, product truth and technical QA in their own passes. Record unavailable playback, listening and native-detail review plainly. The user's rejection of the visual level overrides prior internal acceptance.

## When the film showcases a creative system

A capability film has a different proof burden from a single-product campaign. Build a small production matrix linking **each promised capability → shot and frame/time range → performed action → readable consequence → review evidence**. A repeated motif changing scale or pose may unify the film while demonstrating little of the promised range. Inspect the matrix against the actual cut, not only its script or selected posters.

Useful distinctions include:

| Promise | Evidence the film can perform |
| --- | --- |
| UI/feature direction | One understandable input changes a product state, followed by a legible result. |
| Cinematic camera | Translation, occlusion or parallax reveals a relationship that the starting view did not explain. |
| Editorial/stop-motion treatment | Image, type, crop and stepped timing form a deliberate sequence with readable poses. |
| Story and narration | The order and spoken timing make a problem, mechanism and next action easier to understand. |
| Sound/editing | Actual voice, score and effects support the same departures, accents and arrivals as the picture. |

Choose the demonstrations needed for this audience and promise. Several brands, a particular duration, 3D, frequent cuts or an effect count are not universal requirements. Quiet editorial passages can provide contrast and attention; they still need a defined purpose. A visually polished but conservative showcase can fail through insufficient range or consequential motion. Internal readiness and technical validation do not establish that the user has accepted the replacement.
