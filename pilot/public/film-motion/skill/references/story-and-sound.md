# Story and sound

## Research foundation and software-film translation

Pixar/Khan Academy's curriculum separates character wants/needs, obstacles, stakes, story beats, theme, and visual language. The practical transfer is to establish whose work changes and why the change matters, then make that change visible. A software product is often the mechanism; the user and their work supply stakes. This adaptation is our production synthesis, not a Pixar advertising formula. [Pixar curriculum](https://www.khanacademy.org/pixar), [Pixar's collaboration page](https://www.pixar.com/pixar-in-a-box).

Adobe describes J/L cuts as split edits in which sound and image change at different points. Use a sound lead to introduce the next idea or carry a voice across an illustrative product insert; a cut does not require an impact sound. [Adobe's editing guide](https://www.adobe.com/uk/creativecloud/video/discover/j-cut-and-l-cut.html).

## Build an argument before a shot list

Write these in plain sentences:

1. **Starting belief:** what the viewer thinks or does now.
2. **Friction:** the specific obstacle and consequence for this audience.
3. **New mechanism:** what changes and why it can work.
4. **Performed proof:** an observable action and result, supported by product evidence.
5. **Ending belief:** what the viewer can now understand or trust.
6. **Next action:** one appropriate, accurate step.

Select only the argument the film can support within the chosen duration. A claim such as “everything connected” needs a visible relationship; “faster” needs a truthful comparison or a narrower demonstration. Showing a result without its cause can feel magical while explaining little. Showing every click can make a proof tedious. Include the consequential steps and compress routine ones honestly.

Every beat needs a verb: a queue grows, a person selects, information connects, an error resolves, a result appears. Replace a feature inventory with a causal chain. Ask “because of what?” between beats. When two scenes are only connected by “and then,” decide whether a contrast, consequence, escalation, or deletion would clarify the story.

## Premise and structure options

- **Recognition → mechanism → relief:** begin with a recognizable task, expose the friction, demonstrate the product's intervention, and hold the changed outcome. Useful for feature films and clear launches.
- **Provocation → evidence → reframing:** make an earned challenge to current practice, provide tangible proof, then name the larger possibility. Useful when the brand has a defensible point of view.
- **World → disruption → resolution:** establish a coherent graphic world and its governing relationship, change it through the product's motif, and resolve into identity. Useful for a brand-led launch; include sufficient proof to avoid empty spectacle.
- **Claim → mechanism → proof → objections → offer:** build a VSL in chapters, with each proof answering the current question. Use accurate terms and genuine customer evidence where available. A long script needs progression, not repeated promises.

These are options to adapt, not timed templates. Begin with the ending: the result, emotional state, and action. Use that destination to decide which scenes are necessary. An opening hook should create a question the film actually answers. Avoid a long abstract prologue that spends the entire attention budget before the product appears.

## Recommend the duration

Start from the audience and placement. A familiar user watching one new feature needs less context than a skeptical buyer encountering a new category. A website hero, a social announcement, a sales page, and a founder presentation impose different attention and sound conditions. Select the minimum argument that earns the intended action.

Build a rough time budget from the actual beats: orientation, consequential action, visible response, stable proof, and close. Time a natural scratch read and account for image-only moments and pauses. Reading can overlap narration when both support the same thought, but do not count the same attention twice. Recommend a concrete runtime or narrow range with that rationale, then verify it in the animatic. If it runs long, remove or restructure beats before speeding everything up. Honor an explicit creator runtime; flag an evidence/readability conflict with a concrete cut or scope change.

## Information and emotional rhythm

The [creator-craft study](creator-craft.md) adds a practical planning test: map picture activity, information load and sound density separately. Place the natural read and a temporary musical arc before costly motion where timing depends on them. Replacing the music or retiming action requires a cue/edit reconciliation and new listening/playback, not only a regenerated sheet. A repeated object should return with changed meaning or state.

Plan contrast across the sequence: dense/sparse, near/far, moving/still, warm/cool, intimate/expansive, tension/release. Change one or two dominant dimensions at a time so the viewer can track what matters. Repeated intensity loses emphasis; an expressive shot can earn a still destination.

Copy and image should cooperate. Voice can explain the reason while the image performs the action. Onscreen words can label the result. If all three repeat the same sentence, consider whether the viewer is being asked to read and listen unnecessarily. If they communicate unrelated things, attention may split. Test the actual cut with someone who has not memorized the script.

For text, count from when it becomes stable, visible, and unobscured, not from its first appearance. A familiar one-word label and a novel technical statement need different holds. Judge at the target viewing size with real copy and actual voice; no fixed characters-per-second number proves readability.

## Narration and sound from the animatic onward

For an existing presenter recording or voiceover, follow [speech-led-editing.md](speech-led-editing.md). Build timings from the actual speech edit, retain source/program mappings and versioned word anchors, and give each consequential visual an explicit relationship to the phrase. The [speech/edit model](speech-edit-model.md) keeps semantic review, authored lead/lag and production effort separate from the craft score.

Choose voice posture: founder/direct, expert/plain, intimate, energetic, or intentionally absent. Define pronunciations, product names, sentence stresses, and places to breathe. Record a timed scratch read before locking animation. Word-count divided by a speaking rate is only a planning estimate. Revise the script or edit when the read does not fit; do not speed speech to rescue an overfilled film.

When using connected ElevenLabs media, follow [elevenlabs-production.md](elevenlabs-production.md) for the user's v4 narration preference, live model/voice discovery, locked-script generation, selected-take provenance and reproducible local audio. Its dated production case distinguishes current tool capability from advertised availability; refresh those tools rather than assuming the observed fallback remains necessary.

The cue sheet records shot/frame, voice phrase, music phase, SFX source/action, lead/lag, relative level, and tail. Separate:

- **Music arc:** establish tone, create development, open space for proof, resolve the ending.
- **Object/action sound:** tactile evidence attached to a meaningful event.
- **Transition sound:** continuity or anticipation, with restraint.
- **Ambience/silence:** a world and contrast, not an unfilled gap.

Avoid one whoosh per movement and one impact per cut. Vary sound scale with object/material and camera distance. Let some motions remain silent. Protect voice intelligibility; automate music around speech rather than relying only on a loudness target. Cut on music when the story benefits, and let story lead when a result needs time.

Prepare separate stems when feasible. Review sibilance, clicks, clipping, low-end masking, narration fatigue, tails, stereo/mono behavior, and sync. Listen on more than one relevant playback setup when available. Measure true peak and loudness against the actual destination's current specification; no universal LUFS target suits every platform. A measurement is not a quality judgment. The bundled browser preview is silent, so encoded soundtrack listening requires another playback route.

Captions need accurate wording, timing, line breaks, contrast, and safe placement. They must not obscure the product proof. A silent-social edit may need different pacing/copy, not simply the same cut with audio removed.

When editing a natural read into separated phrases, validate source cuts and fades against the audio signal. ASR word endpoints can include silence or lag into the next phrase. Clamping captions to an edit and retaining the script's word count cannot prove that a consonant survived. Keep source-to-timeline mappings and inspect suspect boundaries; label a source transcription separately from transcription of the edited file. Signal checks, captions and text-only reviews never substitute for listening. Recheck true peak in the encoded video after audio codec conversion.
