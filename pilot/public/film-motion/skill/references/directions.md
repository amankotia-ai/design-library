# Editorial and high motion

Use this to choose a treatment, then author a specific direction. These are production options, not rigid genre templates.

| Decision | Editorial | High motion |
|---|---|---|
| Attention | Layout, cropping, meaningful copy, contrast, stillness | Choreography, transformations, spatial relationships, changes in density |
| Image world | Real product, curated photography/illustration, tactile plates, typography | Graphic systems, isolated UI, motifs, objects, 2.5D/3D where useful |
| Edit | Cuts and held compositions make an argument | Motion carries anchors between states; quiet destinations make it readable |
| Type | Hierarchy and phrasing carry thought | Type can be a subject, mask, spatial plane, or rhythmic accent |
| Camera | Often locked, with selected guided reveals | More expressive, with clear motive, target, and arrival |
| Sound | Voice, intimate detail, deliberate music or silence | Designed transients, movement texture, harmonic arcs, voice where useful |
| Main failure | Elegant but vague; inert slides; tiny evidence | Competing motion; fake complexity; proof hidden by transition |

For either direction define:

- **Thesis:** a sentence explaining how the visual world expresses the product's promise. Example: a collaboration product organizes many independent fragments into one shared working surface. This motivates composition and transition without claiming the real UI morphs that way.
- **Motif:** one recurring shape, relationship, gesture, material, or typographic behavior. Let its meaning develop rather than repeat unchanged.
- **Palette roles:** stage, subject, accent, text, and transitions. Choose a limited set of related grounds; test adjacent shots, contrast, saturated color compression, and the trajectory of color over the film. A continuous palette can connect a cut; a deliberate break can mark a new chapter.
- **Type:** family/source, hierarchy, line breaks, optical alignment, spacing, word grouping, and safe areas. Make exact campaign copy distinguishable from native UI.
- **Motion grammar:** preferred interpolation, acceleration character, overshoot behavior, layering/stagger logic, transformation classes, and purposeful exceptions. Do not choose one easing for every property.
- **Finish:** edge quality, material, grain/noise, contrast, light, bloom, blur, and treatment of pristine UI. Define what each layer receives.
- **Sound:** voice posture, motif, music arc, transient density, negative space, and endings.

Prepare representative frames for the opening, a proof scene, and the ending. One frame can be excellent while the direction fails as a sequence; also demonstrate the join that establishes the film's motion language.

## Match purpose without forcing structure

**Brand launch:** give the product a memorable point of view; move from a recognizable tension toward a new possibility, substantiate it, and resolve into identity/action. A logo is a resolution when the preceding world gives it meaning.

**Feature highlight:** select one consequential workflow. Establish its starting state, perform the relevant action, expose the changed result, and show the benefit in context. Feature names alone do not explain value.

**VSL:** build a sustained, credible sales argument. Clarify who the product helps and why current approaches are costly; explain the mechanism; demonstrate proof; address material objections; present the offer and an accurate next step. Use genuine evidence and terms. Do not fabricate scarcity, testimonials, or return-on-investment claims. Longer duration does not justify more visual noise.

## Hybrid discipline

A calm editorial setup can establish the world before a high-motion reveal; a high-motion brand opening can settle into lucid proof. State the handoff in the treatment and preserve common typography, palette, object identity, and sound. A transition should connect two ideas or alter perspective, not merely fill the seam.
