## Original software-film transfer proposals

These are this project's proposed applications, not Schiffer's own app-film advice or tested implementations:

1. **Plan entities, actions and sequence separately.** First list truthful product entities and states; then valid actions/results; then select the argument's order. Do not start with a transition inventory and invent product behavior to fit it.
2. **Give the transition an action and a spatial reason.** A camera descent can follow an object becoming active, then let a real compositional edge carry the attention handoff. In software, a panel edge, card boundary or approved graphic plane can serve that function without pretending the app itself has hidden geometry.
3. **Preserve enough shared context.** The transition should connect relevant states. A mask can conceal an edit; it cannot prove that an unrelated result belongs to the same user/task/data. UI designer supplies before/action/result evidence and the persistent identifier.
4. **Use stable coverage as the destination.** A demanding move should lead into readable proof. Compare the same beat with a direct cut and a locked arrival; keep motion only when its comprehension or emotional benefit survives that comparison.
5. **Stage and test both halves together.** Motion designer and cinematographer agree on outgoing velocity, crop, boundary shape and incoming composition before rendering. Editor checks the relationship in the whole cut. The compositor owns edge/blur integration; render engineer owns deterministic frame seeking and export fidelity.

Required experiment: create departure → action → edge crossover → arrival → readable result sheets, plus a consecutive-frame strip around the crossover. Inspect at delivery size, then play at original speed and listen separately. Record which method wins and why. No universal timing, constant camera movement, speed ramp or sound effect is prescribed by this study.
