## Proposed software-film experiments

These are our applications, not descriptions of a Gawx software production:

- Give an ordinary task a tangible obstacle and visible resolution. A setup can introduce a repeated object or state; its return should convey a change, rather than merely repeat attractive footage.
- Alternate useful detail with enough context to locate the action. Preserve the same case/data/object through a transition; avoid close-ups that hide what actually changed.
- Plan light, material and palette roles before grading. Compare a static frame with the same action under a camera move. In a software renderer, camera motion and lighting are separate controls; neither repairs unclear product proof.
- Treat a stylistic peak as a passage with an entrance and a quieter destination. Protect stable text/proof intervals in both editorial and high-motion treatments.
- Choose whether the close resolves the established graphic language or uses a deliberate simplification/value break. Measure actual legibility and hold duration in the delivery format; do not copy this film's timing as a preset. The visible YouTube plaque establishes brand presence before the white ending, without establishing how that prop/image was produced.

Owner mapping: director/story establish the obstacle and return; art/UI/camera establish readable states; motion/editor connect them; sound/narrator time and audition the actual cut; color/render/reviewer verify the encoded result. The 39-sheet scan informs those decisions without clearing their production checks.
