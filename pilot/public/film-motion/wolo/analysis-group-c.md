# Wolo Worm and Okbet film studies

These two launch films solve different editorial problems. Worm moves from a provocative category setup into detailed trading UI, pauses for a terminal-style integration story, and finishes with rapid brand variations. Okbet makes a single chat conversation the film: a question, the visible answer, a transaction and a reversal. Their shared technique is to enlarge the part of the interface that carries the current idea, then return to enough context to keep the sequence understandable.

Review used full-duration sampled frame sequences and three consecutive-frame windows. It did not include original-speed playback or audio listening; statements about soundtrack, beat sync, perceived smoothness or voice delivery would require those checks. Time ranges below describe visible sections and are approximate to the sampling interval, except the explicit dense windows. [Coverage](review-group-c.json) records the inspected sheets and exact source frame ranges.

### Editing decisions to retain

The film alternates between three scales: category/world, interface context, and individual control. That gives close-ups a purpose. The leverage action is especially instructive: the film shows which panel contains the control before magnifying it, tracks the parameter change, and returns to the wider panel so the next action is legible.

The energy is uneven by design. The opening and identity coda use many texture changes; the terminal section stays spatially restrained while text accumulates. A synthesis that called this film uniformly fast would miss its most conspicuous structural contrast.

Hard cuts do substantial work. White flashes and strong light/dark inversions punctuate changes in visual register. Continuous transformations are reserved for selected objects and reframing. There is no reason to manufacture a morph at every join.

### Motion and art direction

A largely black/charcoal stage unifies UI, terminal text and object marks. Green is both an interaction/state accent in the interface and the terminal's monochrome treatment. Yellow/orange highlights distinguish the Bitcoin/chart segment. Warmer footage supplies a different emotional and historical register without changing the product palette itself.

The film combines tightly spaced sans serif copy, exaggerated serif newspaper type, and small monospaced terminal text. These type choices distinguish modes of communication, not merely levels in one heading hierarchy. Perspective and soft focus make UI appear as a staged surface; the important control is brought closer and made larger at the moment it matters.

The dense leverage window, source frames 675–719, shows a separate enlarged insert and a return to the wider panel. The hand closes over the slider as the value changes, then opens on release. The values briefly dip from 1.0 toward 0.9 before rising to 5.0. This is observable behavior; an exact spring or easing function cannot be recovered from these images alone.

The identity window, source frames 1290–1391, shows many variants held for roughly two source frames at 30 fps. Their shared location and recognizable outline are what make that speed possible. The material sequence is not a safe model for explanatory text or fine product detail.

## Okbet Launch

[Full source film](https://media.wolo.digital/work/okbet-launch.mp4?v=557fa4b1) · [Frame viewer](studies/okbet-launch/index.html)

37.674 seconds, 1920 × 1080, 24 fps, 904 decoded frames. Inspected all 151 samples at every sixth source frame, spanning 0–37.5 seconds, on seven sheets. Additional consecutive-frame review covers 13.50–14.50 seconds, where the phone turns from screen to back and returns to its screen.

### Editing decisions to retain

The real unit of the edit is the conversation turn. Each question gets space beside the device; each answer gets a closer inspection of the screen. The film has a stable visual grammar that viewers can learn after the first exchange. Later turns can therefore move more efficiently without losing their structure.

The sequence preserves causal continuity: ask, receive choices, select, confirm, see completion, then reverse the operation. This makes the launch feel like a small demonstration rather than a feature list. The closing joke works because the functional explanation has already landed.

The text's pacing is visibly staged word by word, but no claim is made that it follows narration or music; the audio was not reviewed. The filmed UI is shown as evidence of the workflow depicted, not as verification of a live product's current behavior.

### Motion and art direction

The white background is almost completely empty. Black sans serif text, the green chat surface, warm metal phone edges and small yellow/orange identity accents supply nearly all the color. A single device is enough to anchor the entire film. Its slight perspective and occasional rotation add object presence without requiring a large 3D environment.

The main motion vocabulary is small: type-on, soft blur/fade out, lateral device relocation, scale/reframing, restrained device tilt, conversation growth and one full turn. Variation comes from composition and the next question, not a new effect on every shot.

The consecutive-frame turn at source frames 324–347 confirms an actual visible progression through front, narrow edge, back and front; it is not a simple front/back cut inferred from sparse samples. The turn also changes the device's scale and position before it settles beside the next line of copy. The settled front view lasts longer than the profile/back exposure, protecting the screen's role as the readable destination.

## Transferable rules from these two films

1. Establish where a detail belongs before enlarging it. Return to context after the parameter or state changes.
2. Let content determine the pace. A small status change, a full chat answer and a material-logo burst require different exposure times.
3. Keep one invariant during fast change: the device, the central silhouette, the cursor's target or the conversation state.
4. Assign typography by communication mode. Editorial commentary, instructional copy and terminal output can have distinct type treatments while sharing a stage.
5. Use camera/framing change to answer a question: what should be read, what was selected, what changed, or how two things connect.
6. Build the demonstration as an action chain. The completion state is part of the story, not an optional decorative end frame.
7. Reserve visual excess for moments that can tolerate it. Two-frame material changes work for a recognizable mark; detailed answers need a stable close view.
8. Decide the film's governing art direction early. Worm permits mixed media under a dark/CRT system; Okbet obtains coherence through an almost empty white stage and one recurring object.
