# Wolo SpaceX Cornbread and Nsave visual studies

Reviewed 11 November 2026. These are the complete public lightbox films on the [Wolo work page](https://wolo.digital/work/), not its preview loops. All approximately four-per-second overview samples were inspected, with the consecutive-frame windows detailed below. Each study indexes every decoded source frame and preserves source PTS in `frames.csv`. Continuous original-speed playback, audio listening, and a full-resolution finishing review were not performed. Beat boundaries are approximate unless a decoded-frame boundary is supplied. On-screen historical and commercial statements are content of the films, not independently verified facts.

## SpaceX IPO

[Full source](https://media.wolo.digital/work/spacex.mp4?v=2c5007e3) · [Frame viewer](studies/spacex/index.html). 1920 × 1080; 30000/1001 fps; 1,195 decoded frames; 39.893 seconds container duration. All 171 every-seventh-frame samples were inspected through seven overview sheets, plus 60 consecutive frames in a three-sheet typography window.

| Approximate seconds | Visible construction | Editorial function |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2.2 | A small portrait sits inside a large white field. Short words appear at its sides; early company imagery replaces and enlarges the inset. | Creates an intimate beginning and reserves a large change of scale for the history that follows. |
| 2.2–5.8 | Full-frame early-company footage, overhead map, street sign, then a familiar search page and typed query. | Moves from personal recollection to location and public curiosity through recognizable image categories. |
| 5.8–10.3 | Cropped news-style pages, a podium scene, and oversized stacked copy beside a person. | Switches from searching to a declaration. News-style imagery is an editorial device here; the depicted claims are not verification of real events. |
| 10.3–14.0 | Small centered archival/technical clips on white return, with tiny word changes within them. | Resets intensity and creates a common frame for visually disparate material. |
| 14.0–23.6 | Rocket engine detail, launch views, stage presentation, launchpad, cheering people, and orbital images. | Expands from confined windows into spectacle, intercutting machinery with human reaction. |
| 23.6–26.0 | A large two-word title overlays a helmet. Its second word is replaced by a solid white bar as the background cuts to launch footage; title and bar persist across further cuts. | Makes an argument through a simple graphic subtraction rather than adding a decorative transition. |
| 26.0–31.6 | Rocket and exhaust imagery returns; lunar and planetary views are briefly contained on white. | Alternates immersive action with quieter editorial panels. |
| 31.6–34.3 | A bright exhaust plume washes toward a white field. A sentence sits beside a minimal orbital diagram that enters from the right. | Bridges photographic spectacle and abstract ambition through a shared pale field. |
| 34.3–39.9 | Brief rocket and helmet images, then sparse typed copy and a compact brand symbol on white. | Reduces visual density for the final address and identity hold. |

The art direction joins three treatments: documentary-looking footage, a restrained white editorial stage, and technical graphic elements. Small monospaced-looking uppercase copy is the default connective tissue; a few unusually large phrases become emphasis. The typography alternates between centered labels, left/right words around a portrait, and a stacked block beside the subject. It does not require a uniform lower-third template.

Editing supplies most of the energy. Different shots replace one another while a common text anchor or inset position gives continuity. Moving footage, editorial scale changes, and a diagram should not be collapsed into one claim about 3D camera work. The film repeatedly changes the picture's occupied area: small window, full bleed, small window, full bleed, near-empty ending.

**Consecutive window, f735–794 / 24.5245–26.4931 seconds.** All 60 thumbnails in `windows/spacex-type-mask/sheets/00000.jpg`–`00002.jpg` were inspected. At f743, the second word is already fully replaced by the white rectangle simultaneously with a background cut. There is no visible progressive wiping animation in this window. The rectangle and retained first word remain stable through subsequent footage cuts, then both disappear at another shot change. The useful principle is a semantic replacement held across a montage, not an elaborate morph.

Transferable decisions: use picture size as a pacing variable; let a word operation express the central argument; keep a stable graphic anchor across unrelated shots; reserve negative space for a decisive ending. The soundtrack and spoken wording may govern the edit, but that alignment is not established by these still sequences.

## Slash Cornbread

[Full source](https://media.wolo.digital/work/slash-cornbread.mp4?v=530cf79a) · [Frame viewer](studies/slash-cornbread/index.html). 1440 × 1080; 24 fps; 1,847 decoded frames; 77.013 seconds container duration. All 308 every-sixth-frame samples were inspected through 13 overview sheets, plus 48 consecutive frames in a two-sheet collage window.

| Approximate seconds | Visible construction | Editorial function |
|---|---|---|
| 0–6.0 | Period-looking music performances, children, city streets, and a presenter. Small conventional subtitles stay near the bottom. | Establishes a cultural era and setting before the central person is introduced. |
| 6.0–8.0 | Graph-paper collage assembles a person, architecture, paper fragments, large handwriting, red circles, crown, and question mark. | Turns the introduction into an authored question with a recognizable material vocabulary. |
| 8.0–16.5 | Monochrome street and transit material; portrait, handwritten name, layered newspaper cuttings, and graffiti close-ups. | Provides social setting, identifies the subject, and establishes the mark as the recurring object. |
| 16.5–24.8 | A woman's portrait receives a handwritten name and small red heart. Street footage alternates with a handwritten dedication, ending on a torn-paper version on a wall. | Gives the mark a specific human motivation; writing is both story content and graphic treatment. |
| 25.0–35.2 | Short era collage, transport map, buses, tagged walls, street footage, photographs, and a close view of a printed portrait. | Builds repetition across places. The marks gain meaning because they recur in a social environment. |
| 35.2–42.6 | Another paper collage with a portrait, clippings and red loops; footage of the older artist painting; the image darkens into a brief near-black pause. | Consolidates recognition and gives the story a chapter boundary. |
| 42.6–50.8 | Color interview and painting footage, wall-scale marks and a seated portrait. | Shifts from historical reconstruction toward a living person and his actions. |
| 50.8–65.7 | Present-day entrepreneurs, laptops, warehouses, parcels, collaborative work, camera equipment, and construction. | Transfers the story's spirit into the brand's audience and working worlds. This is the major brand pivot, much later than in a direct product explainer. |
| 65.7–73.0 | Cities, concrete work, finishing surfaces, and bridge/skyline views. | Broadens the metaphor from individual marks to creating opportunities and physical worlds. |
| 73.0–77.0 | Pale cream end card; the Slash name draws in a graffiti-like hand, then holds with small disclosure text below. | Resolves the same writing motif into identity. |

The 4:3 frame, aged-looking archive material, monochrome passages, paper grain, beige tape, torn edges, and imperfect handwriting make a coherent material world. Red is used sparingly for annotation: loops, underlines, arrows and a heart. Later contemporary footage is comparatively muted and warm, with deep shadows, natural windows and brick/wood interiors. The modern section drops most of the collage effects and lets people working carry the argument.

The edit alternates historical footage with designed editorial pauses; it is not a constant stream of kinetic type. It also separates subtitles from authored lettering. Subtitles carry the continuous verbal thread, while large written names and marks receive special attention. Frequent ordinary cuts, modest reframing of stills, and occasional soft/blurred transitions do most of the work. The recurring mark bridges media more strongly than any one transition preset.

**Consecutive window, f144–191 / 6.0000–7.9583 seconds.** All 48 thumbnails in `windows/slash-cornbread-collage-reveal/sheets/00000.jpg`–`00001.jpg` were inspected. Photographic cutouts settle onto a faint grid while black handwriting reveals in short increments. Red curved strokes expand around the central portrait; a crown and large question mark join; a red ellipse is drawn around the written name. The entire composition then cuts to monochrome transit footage. These are layered arrivals and drawn marks, not evidence of a continuously tracked physical camera or paper simulation.

Transferable decisions: choose a metaphor from the subject's real activity; carry it through imagery, annotation and the end identity; use a limited set of tactile materials; allow a brand film to earn its late pivot through a complete cultural story. A direct feature launch would require an earlier mechanism and proof, so this timing should not be copied indiscriminately.

## Nsave Referrals

[Full source](https://media.wolo.digital/work/nsave-referrals.mp4?v=8a1e1c41) · [Frame viewer](studies/nsave-referrals/index.html). 1920 × 1080; 60 fps; 2,389 decoded frames; 39.829 seconds container duration. All 160 every-fifteenth-frame samples were inspected through seven overview sheets, plus 120 consecutive frames in a five-sheet referral-diagram window.

| Approximate seconds | Visible construction | Editorial function |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2.6 | Black type arrives on white in short units; the monetary hook counts upward and emojis change expression beside it. | Gives one concrete reason to pay attention, with casual social tone. |
| 2.6–6.3 | Large cropped currency symbols give way to short invitation phrases, red brand text, a handshake and a red highlight behind the program name. | Connects the hook to the referral mechanism and establishes emphasis colors. |
| 6.3–10.5 | A short sentence leads into a red looping line, an emoji at its crest, then a simple branching tree with three people and reward labels. | Converts a verbal proposition into a sequential visual explanation. |
| 10.5–17.5 | A large plus sign, deposit condition, changing numbers, red drawn arrow, and a larger benefit phrase. | Builds the next condition and reward in a separate clause rather than crowding one diagram. |
| 17.5–24.8 | Excited emoji, five human icons added in sequence, then a separate bonus phrase and money-with-wings graphic. | Makes a count and threshold tangible before stating the next reward. |
| 25.0–28.6 | Notification bars accumulate around a growing central dollar total, ending at the amount displayed in the film. | Turns individual rewards into perceived accumulation and provides the visual climax. |
| 28.6–34.0 | A phone message view rises under short sharing instructions. The composition then fades toward white. | Gives a recognizable next action after the benefits. |
| 34.0–39.8 | Red wordmark holds on white, then fades out. | Ends with sustained low density after the accumulation scene. |

The design is intentionally sparse: white field, large black sans serif type, red brand/emphasis elements, occasional gray system notifications, and familiar emoji. Most scenes have one central idea, often two lines of copy, with an icon or line doing one supporting job. There is little need for texture, cinematic lighting, or elaborate perspective. Lettering and icon scale establish hierarchy.

Motion includes word/character build, directional sliding, momentary directional blur, scale change, changing numeric values, line drawing, icon arrivals and a final fade. Many transitions move the entire phrase or diagram as a unit. The animation is playful, but repeated settled compositions keep its structure clear. No exact easing, overshoot magnitude, or authoring software is established by these images.

**Consecutive window, f450–569 / 7.5000–9.4833 seconds.** All 120 thumbnails in `windows/nsave-referrals-referral-tree/sheets/00000.jpg`–`00004.jpg` were inspected. A red line first draws beside settled copy. The text moves left with visible blur as the line and lead emoji enter. The curved line moves away while the lead emoji stays above a new branching diagram. The first, second and third lower emojis arrive at different times, with reward labels and small accent bursts. The connector strokes also extend in phases; the full tree is not revealed in one uniform fade. The useful structure is statement → guided handoff → actor → repeated consequences.

Transferable decisions: partition offer, condition, bonus and next action into distinct visual clauses; show counts by adding countable objects; use accumulation for the climax; keep the final action concrete. The referral amounts are source-film copy, not a current offer or recommended promise to reuse.

## Shared craft across these three

All three use density changes and a restrained ending, but their main engines differ. SpaceX uses scale and montage to build ambition. Cornbread uses a cultural story and a writing motif to connect identity and audience. Nsave uses sequential typography and diagrams to explain an offer. The transferable common rule is to choose a small visual vocabulary that can perform several narrative jobs, then change its density and scale as the argument advances.
