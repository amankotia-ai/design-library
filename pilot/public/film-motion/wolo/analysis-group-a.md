# Velo and Slash film studies

Source: the full public lightbox films linked from [Wolo Work](https://wolo.digital/work/), accessed 11 November 2026. These observations come from consecutive overview samples across each complete film plus three targeted consecutive-frame windows. All timestamps are source PTS; frame indices are zero-based. Overview beat boundaries are approximate brackets, not an edit decision list. Audio and original-speed playback were not reviewed.

## Velo

Source: [Velo full film](https://media.wolo.digital/work/velo.mp4?v=2da27831). Video stream: 1920×1080, 24fps, 868 decoded frames, 36.166667 seconds; container including audio is 36.223129 seconds. All 145 every-sixth-frame samples were inspected in six 320px-thumbnail sheets, plus all 72 consecutive frames f84–155 in three nominally 480px-thumbnail sheets. Larger sheets were resized by the viewing tool. Exact coverage is in `review-group-a.json`.

Velo constructs a continuous product world. Colored strands introduced before the product recur as connectors, spatial guide rails and orbiting accents; the same pale light and glass-like materials connect abstract branding, actual interface panels and the closing reward token.

| Approximate source seconds | Reference frames | Observed beat |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4.2 | f0–100 | Fine colored strokes wander over white with very soft pastel patches; the strands become thicker and crowd the left side before gathering into a horizontal line. |
| 4.2–7.7 | f101–185 | A compact colored waveform develops around a code-like V symbol; its characters resolve into the Velo wordmark. The wordmark then moves off the left as the strands continue across the field. |
| 7.7–10.8 | f186–258 | A spread of four floating UI cards appears. The group tilts and changes scale; a close view of a beveled card edge leads into the dashboard. |
| 10.8–14.5 | f259–348 | A full trading dashboard occupies most of the frame. Oversized translucent green pointer shapes sweep across it; the panel then shrinks and leaves laterally with colored lines still visible. |
| 14.5–19.8 | f349–475 | A short value statement and a single trading panel share a spacious two-part composition. The panel enlarges until its form controls and buy/sell area dominate. The glass-like cursor remains the action cue. |
| 19.8–23.8 | f476–571 | Vertical travel reveals exchange integration cards, followed by a borrowing interface. The sequence shifts from broad product environment to separate capabilities. |
| 23.8–27 | f572–647 | Several category words replace one another, then a unifying message appears. Colored strokes briefly become a loose grid; an oversized dark V token enters from below. |
| 27–35.5 | f648–851 | The token rotates amid colored orbital strands. A compact rewards/date composition holds beside it while the token keeps turning. |
| 35.5–36.17 | f852–867 | The composition fades toward white. |

**Editing and motion.** The structure is reveal → environment → individual capabilities → unifying token. It often hands attention across a scene boundary through an already established line, edge or direction. The pale background makes lateral departures feel continuous. Large changes in view scale alternate with readable, relatively settled compositions. These samples establish motion paths and staging, but not the original easing functions or precise 3D camera setup.

**Dense window f84–155, 3.500–6.458 seconds.** The messy strands move out of the central horizontal region; at f100 the field is almost a straight baseline. At f101 a compact waveform and the V-like symbol appear near the right-center, then the grouping enlarges and travels toward center. The angled characters yield to the wordmark while the colored wave structure remains. This is visual continuity through a shared baseline and color vocabulary, not evidence that all text is one geometrically continuous morph.

**Art direction.** White dominates. Pastel cyan, pink, yellow and lavender behave like reflected light rather than large opaque brand fills. UI cards have rounded corners, thin dark boundaries, bevel-like edges and translucent color. The repeated green cursor is deliberately much larger than a functional screen cursor, giving a clear foreground actor. The charcoal token at the end concentrates contrast after a mostly luminous sequence. Black sans-serif copy remains short and alternates between side captions and center statements.

**Transferable rules.** Give a product film one persistent material or path motif. Establish the world, then enlarge one useful action; return to a simple object at the close. Assign one conspicuous object the job of directing attention. Limit simultaneous text, camera movement and UI changes when a capability must be read. The source's final date line visibly contains placeholder-like `xxx` values; this is a craft reference, not approved product information to reuse.

## Slash Global

Source: [Slash Global full film](https://media.wolo.digital/work/slash-global.mp4?v=876bd751). 1440×1080, 24fps, 648 decoded frames, 27 seconds. All 108 every-sixth-frame samples were inspected in five sheets; the 72 consecutive frames f60–131 were inspected in three larger sheets.

Slash Global uses a rapid associative montage to make cross-border finance feel mobile and contemporary. Text stays near the center while trains, traders, clocks, cars, cash, rockets, newsreaders and cities change beneath it. A short pause in a familiar chat-bubble layout makes the central product benefit more explicit.

| Approximate source seconds | Reference frames | Observed beat |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2.7 | f0–64 | Large cropped identity over transit imagery gives way to a small rounded central text chip. Letter substitutions and fine crosshair-like guides accompany short city/train cuts. |
| 2.7–5.3 | f65–127 | Black-and-white trading-floor images populate a four-quadrant field. Watch details progressively replace them and the watch finally fills the frame. |
| 5.3–8.25 | f128–197 | The circular watch yields to a tachometer, then racing, cash and roulette imagery. The center chip carries the argument across the changing footage; a field of tiny money pictograms forms a brief graphic break. |
| 8.25–10.6 | f198–254 | Two blue message bubbles appear over an upward view of skyscrapers. This is a longer stable benefit statement within the surrounding rapid montage. |
| 10.6–14.7 | f255–352 | A brief graphic flash leads into engine/rocket footage, a road and an overhead crowd with bright tracked-looking blocks. Short financial action words change within the same central label system. |
| 14.7–16.8 | f353–403 | One news presenter becomes four and then a dense newsreader mosaic. The multiplication visualizes geographic breadth. |
| 16.8–21.5 | f404–515 | Cities and transit resume, then payment-card close-ups and urban imagery alternate. The product appears as a tactile object within the same film language. |
| 21.5–24 | f516–575 | A small Earth on black has a URL wrapped around its equator; the globe grows before the closing title sequence. |
| 24–27 | f576–647 | Alternating black and warm-white title fields reduce the montage to a compact verbal identity, ending on the small Slash logo. |

**Editing and motion.** Content changes carry most of the energy. Central label continuity means the viewer need not repeatedly search for the argument. Montage clusters have local semantic roles: speed, work, exchange, borders and payment. The newsreader multiplication is a density escalation rather than a new arbitrary effect. There are brief exaggerated type flashes and character scrambling, but these are punctuation around simpler compositions.

**Dense window f60–131, 2.500–5.458 seconds.** After the city shot, a trading image appears in the lower-left quadrant; successive quadrants fill with other trading-floor imagery. The watch first replaces the lower-left tile, then also appears in the upper-right. The remaining people images disappear as the watch expands to full field. An oversized word flashes over the watch immediately before the tachometer. The round dial-to-dial relation survives the hard image replacement. This is a specific example of using a modular layout as an intermediate edit state and ending with a graphic match.

**Art direction.** The 4:3 frame creates a compact editorial presentation. Grain, motion-blurred transport and warm metallic shots contrast with archival monochrome trading images. The small contemporary sans-serif label is usually black on a white rounded chip; occasional inversions keep it legible. Fine vertical/horizontal guide lines add an interface layer without turning the footage into a software mockup. Blue chat bubbles are deliberately familiar. The closing palette collapses to black, off-white and the serif Slash identity.

**Transferable rules.** Hold one anchor stable while changing the surrounding medium. Build montage in concept clusters, not a random library of impressive shots. Insert a slower, unmistakable benefit layout after several dense cuts. Use a shape correspondence—such as dial to dial—to connect different subjects. Return to product evidence before the logo, so a broad mood piece still has a tangible offering.

## Slash Fourth of July

Source: [Slash Fourth of July full film](https://media.wolo.digital/work/slash-4-july.mp4?v=f16879fa). 1920×1080, 30fps, 1,343 decoded frames, 44.766667 seconds. All 168 every-eighth-frame samples were inspected in seven sheets, giving 3.75 samples per second. All 72 consecutive frames f1180–1251 were inspected in three larger sheets.

This film is an archival brand manifesto. Its progression connects immigration, opportunity, inventions and large technological ambitions. Most visible motion comes from the footage and editing. A nearly fixed caption position and unusually condensed serif typography unify material with many different dates, formats and image qualities.

| Approximate source seconds | Reference frames | Observed beat |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2.7 | f0–80 | Archival portraits change inside a small centered photographic area on white. Fine white serif copy lies across the image. |
| 2.7–6.9 | f81–207 | Liberty, arrival/crowd imagery, a political speech and families establish the historical premise. Some frames use rounded film-window edges against black. |
| 6.9–10.7 | f208–320 | Interview, control room, rocket pad and launch imagery shift from early history toward contemporary technical ambition. |
| 10.7–13.3 | f321–399 | Portrait, city, bridge and motorcycle images broaden the motif of arrival, scale and possibility. |
| 13.3–18.9 | f400–567 | An interview is intercut with signs, computing and medical imagery, a small chip and an early smartphone demonstration. The sequence connects rhetoric about invention with objects that embody it. |
| 18.9–26.7 | f568–800 | Speech footage, moon imagery, rocket engineering and aircraft make a sustained ambition/action cluster. |
| 26.7–33.9 | f801–1017 | Founders and technology presentations lead into modern robotics/manufacturing/computing and further launch footage. |
| 33.9–39.2 | f1018–1175 | Speech, travel archive, Liberty and aerospace/space views combine the earlier historical and future-facing strands. A bright fade empties the image field. |
| 39.2–42.4 | f1176–1271 | A small centered sentence builds on white, then the type becomes very large and travels laterally. The final action word receives a US-flag treatment. |
| 42.4–44.77 | f1272–1342 | A small flag-colored Slash mark holds on black. |

**Editing and motion.** The structure is cumulative rather than explanatory: historical people → opportunity → things people made → ambition → brand proposition. Individual source formats are preserved through borders and mats instead of being forced into one clean frame. Frequent cuts within semantic clusters make different eras feel related. The fixed caption axis gives the eye a stable place to return while footage changes. Because audio was not reviewed, this study does not claim narration provenance, music synchronization or an exact speech-led cut method.

**Dense window f1180–1251, 39.333–41.700 seconds.** Words accumulate in a small sentence at center. At f1208 the composition jumps abruptly to much larger type with the left part cropped offscreen. It then moves laterally, exposing the ending of the sentence. The italic final word enters with a flag-colored treatment and remains comparatively stable as the preceding black words drift. This is an abrupt scale change followed by translation, not a long continuous zoom. Its force depends on the prior film's restrained caption size.

**Art direction.** An extremely narrow, high-contrast serif becomes the throughline. It is usually white over footage, small and centered; the final typography reverses to black on white and grows dramatically. Grain, limited-resolution archive, tinted color footage, rounded film windows and white photographic mats form a historical collage. Color builds through the source material rather than a uniform saturation treatment. The small multicolor mark at the end draws its palette from the flag treatment introduced moments earlier.

**Transferable rules.** A montage can earn coherence through a stable typographic system and an ordered argument even when source aesthetics vary. Keep archive treatments consistent enough to read as intentional. Save the largest typographic change for the key proposition. Use a final motif that has already appeared, so the logo feels like the conclusion of the film's idea. Preserve the difference between imagery used as emotional association and imagery that actually proves a product claim.

## Patterns across these three films

The shared discipline is continuity of attention, not one uniform visual style. Velo carries the viewer with colored lines and a foreground cursor; Global uses central chips, graphic matches and modular image multiplication; Fourth of July uses a fixed caption axis and historical argument. Each film reduces its visual vocabulary near the ending. The useful production decision is to choose the continuity device first, then vary footage, scene scale and density around it.
